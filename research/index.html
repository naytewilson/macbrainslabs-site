02Quantized transformer execution

A central experiment builds a 28-layer Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct path around four-bit lookup-table weights and native Core ML graph generation. The objective is more precise than “use the Neural Engine”: keep attention, projection, normalization and KV-state machinery in ANE-compatible tensor form and demonstrate the exact assigned device for the resulting operations.

Two Qwen artifacts appear in this record. Placement was measured on the production ANEMLL Core ML conversion of the 28-layer LUT4 model (qwen25_monolithic_full_lut4); perplexity was measured on the hand-authored SwiftMIL model, which runs as two 14-layer halves sharing KV. The production model's infer function had 2,090 operations assigned to ANE and 79 assigned to CPU. The residual CPU assignments belong to the language-model-head argmax tail. Separately, the tested stateful KV primitives (slice_update, read_state, write_state and slice_by_index) were assigned to ANE. A CPU tail does not invalidate a measured ANE-resident state path, and the state path does not justify calling the whole graph 100% ANE.

state_ops = {
  slice_update:    56 / 56  # ANE / total
  read_state:      57 / 57
  write_state:     56 / 56
  slice_by_index: 225 / 225

infer.ops: ANE=2090 | CPU=79
observation: MLComputePlan assignment

Critical compiler constraints include FP16 rank-four graph geometry, integer gather indices, and append-only handling of Core ML's external weight blobs. See the Qwen field note for the failure cases that established those rules.

03Measured batch throughput

The batch sweep measures aggregate throughput on the A18 Pro test system, not individual interactive-user token rate. Each batch value came from a compiled graph with matching physical input dimensions.

Batch=4 is the measured efficiency knee for this setup. The sweep timed single-function probe artifacts; their output quality was not validated, and the batch geometry had not yet been carried into a deployable model. A previously proposed “57.3 tok/s bandwidth ceiling” was later falsified by a batch-K-per-dispatch sweep on a probe model and by a LUT2 experiment that reduced weights without increasing throughput. The updated result distinguishes low-depth dispatch overhead from compute cost at 28 layers. Read the analysis.

04Liquid architecture parity

A separate LFM block-pair S2 production artifact was evaluated under .cpuOnly and .cpuAndNeuralEngine and compared with a pinned FP16 fixture. Placement classified 743/743 compute operations on ANE for that sealed artifact. 05What the results do not say

Boundary conditions

These findings do not establish ANE residency for arbitrary transformer architectures, every token-processing operation, or every Core ML/compiler release. Assigned compute unit is an API-visible planner result. Numeric parity and measured end-to-end speed are distinct experiments with their own conditions.

Research is ongoing. Hypotheses, performance projections, and experimental surrogate shapes are kept separate from hardware-qualified measurements. Negative results and retractions remain part of the record, rather than being silently replaced by favorable headlines.
