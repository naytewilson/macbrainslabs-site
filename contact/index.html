Scope and independence

MacBrains Labs is my independent research name, and I use it for hands-on engineering work. I don’t present it as a separately incorporated company, a university department, an Apple partner or a licensed laboratory. The public technical results describe experiments I ran on systems I own and on authorized software artifacts. They aren’t an endorsement or certification by Apple or by model developers.

I look into graph construction and inference backends, MLComputePlan placement, native Apple Silicon measurement, model quantization, reproducible benchmark methods and runtime reliability. I also work on authorized defensive assessment of software under my control.

Asking about a result

If you write to me about a result, please name the model, graph or function, the claimed measurement, your reproduction environment and any observation that doesn’t match. The public site summarizes selected records. Not every underlying source artifact or model file can be publicly distributed.

Security and testing limits

Publishing this site doesn’t authorize testing of third-party systems. Any defensive security work or vulnerability analysis needs authorization from the system owner, and it stays within that authorization.

Where I work

Maine, United States. I do the research on Apple Silicon development hardware and the authorized compute infrastructure connected to it. I can give physical and legal contact details to appropriate, verified counterparties through private channels when that’s required. They aren’t listed on the public website.
