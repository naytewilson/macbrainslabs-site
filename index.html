Research folio / 001Compiler placement

Qwen2.5 · 1.5B · 28 layers · LUT4

A preference is not
a placement proof.

Inspect the compiler’s assignment before making a claim about the hardware.

2,090ANE-assigned operations
79CPU-assigned operations

What this establishes

MLComputePlan classified these operations as ANE- or CPU-assigned on the named compiled artifact. The 79 CPU-assigned operations are the language-model-head argmax output tail. The later shipped infer build reports 2,147 ANE / 82 CPU; it is a different build, not a replacement label for this measurement.

Boundary of the claim

Compiler assignment does not establish runtime residency, throughput or end-to-end model quality.

Research folio / 002Numerical parity

LFM2.5 · 1.2B · Layer slices → full chain

Every slice passed.
The chain did not.

A useful negative result changes what gets tested next.

SliceSliceSlice…
16-layer chainParity gate missed

What was observed

Individual layer slices and the final head meet a 0.005 relative-L2 parity gate on the Neural Engine. The composed 16-layer chain and its token logits do not.

Boundary of the claim

Local numerical agreement cannot be promoted into a claim of complete-model correctness.

Research folio / 003Performance

A18 Pro · Decode · Batch sweeps

The limit that
wasn’t the limit.

When the measurements disagree with the explanation, change the explanation.

Initial hypothesis

A 57.3 tok/s bandwidth ceiling.

What the measurements showed

Smaller weights did not speed it up.
More work per dispatch did.

The investigation

The ceiling came from dividing a compiler planning figure by the weight-file size. Smaller LUT2 weights gave no matching speedup, and a per-dispatch batch sweep moved past the supposed limit, so it was retracted. At 28-layer production depth, per-layer compute dominates more of each step than fixed dispatch cost.

Boundary of the claim

The batch sweep timed probe artifacts whose output quality was not validated. A performance result belongs to its device, build and execution conditions, not to every model or workload.

Independent by design / 04

Build. Experiment.
Understand what’s
underneath.

MacBrains Labs is an independent research notebook on Apple Silicon: how programs are compiled, where work is assigned, and what the results can actually support. Open methods and explicit limits are part of the work, not footnotes to it.

We do not claim Apple affiliation, privileged ANE access, or universal placement results. Individual measurements refer to the named artifact, backend, tensor shape and hardware configuration.

Researcher: Nayte Wilson (ORCID iD 0009-0003-4026-8212). The public measurement summaries are archived at Zenodo under DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23798037; cite that version DOI. An archived DOI is not peer review.
