Did either graft pay off?

Both grafts passed their numerical checks. The ring graft’s compiler plan placed nothing on the Neural Engine. In the timed build, the EAGLE graft made every call slower, and it has to earn that back through accepted drafts. Correctness, placement and cost are separate checks.

Correct, but planned for the CPU

The ring graft is a speculative-attention graph for a ring-buffer key/value cache. Each call writes two provisional key/value proposals. It takes an acceptance count from the host. Then it attends only over committed history, including after the ring wraps.

On 27 August 2026 it passed its end-to-end check at two cache sizes. Rejected proposals that were physically in the cache stayed out of attention in every poison case.

Those runs requested the Neural Engine. A separate placement census of the exact compiled artifacts then asked MLComputePlan where each operation goes. For every operation that has a device, the answer was the CPU, at both sizes, under both .cpuAndNeuralEngine and .all.

The 43 operations with no device are constants. The census reports Core ML’s preferred placement. It isn’t a trace of execution.

A correct draft head, slower per call

An EAGLE-3 draft head was grafted into the single-token function of a LUT4 Qwen2.5 model, so one call returns both the verified token and a draft. On 24 June 2026 the grafted subgraph matched the PyTorch reference at three internal stages, on two independent inputs: relative L2 at most 0.00195 and cosine at least 0.999998.

The graft isn’t free. Here’s a breakdown of one build, timed as the fastest of 120 calls on the same Mac:

BuildFastest callAdded
Base, graft removed by the compiler20.83 msNone
Graft, draft logits out24.66 ms+3.83 ms
Graft with on-device argmax27.41 ms+2.75 ms

That build added 6.58 ms, or 31.6%, per call. The draft logits account for +3.83 ms of that, and the on-device argmax over a 151,936-token vocabulary adds +2.75 ms. The argmax runs as 17 CPU operations.

A separate settled-loop measurement, with a different harness, recorded 18.98 ms per base call and 22.98 ms per graft call. Draft acceptance was 63.16% open-loop and 65.66% against an oracle, over 80 rounds on 3 prompts. A graft call that yields about 1.6 tokens can make up for a call that’s about 21% longer. The tokens-per-second figures in that receipt are calculated from those two measurements, not timed generation, so I don’t quote them as a speedup.

The one timed generation run forced every draft to be accepted: 65.1 against 37.7 tokens per second, over three 50-token trials. That’s an upper bound, not a result at real acceptance.

The fixed cost of each call

The cost of a call shows up in a simpler test. Five fixed-shape builds of a two-layer LFM2.5 slice processed 1, 4, 8, 16 and 64 tokens per call. Each ran 21 timed calls after one warmup, with seeded random inputs.

Tokens per callOpsFastest callMedian callPer token
115114.09 ms15.14 ms14.09 ms
477115.16 ms15.96 ms3.79 ms
879914.42 ms15.19 ms1.80 ms
1685514.89 ms15.76 ms0.93 ms
64119115.26 ms16.21 ms0.24 ms

A 64-fold range of tokens and an eightfold range of operations moved the call time by about a millisecond. At this depth, the call is the cost. That’s the cost speculative decoding and batching try to amortize. It’s also why a graft that adds milliseconds to every call has to earn them back. A full model does far more work per call, so this floor is a property of shallow graphs, as N/003 found for batch sweeps.

What it does and doesn’t show

Takeaway

Prove correctness first. Then check placement with MLComputePlan. Then time the call. A graph can pass the first gate and fail either of the others. Requesting the Neural Engine doesn’t mean the work ran on it.

The ring graft is a negative result for that design as measured: correct, but the compiler plan put none of its operations on the Neural Engine. The EAGLE graft matched its reference numerically. This page doesn’t show a placement count for it, only that the draft’s argmax runs as 17 CPU operations. Its net value is still open until generation is timed at real acceptance. Neither result says what other designs can do.
